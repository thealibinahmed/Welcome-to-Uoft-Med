Starting Year 1

You do not need to arrive having studied ahead.

Give yourself permission to settle in. Nothing on this page is required preparation for your first day.

Start with the course map and early-opportunities section, then return as each part of the curriculum becomes relevant.

Start here

Three courses shape Year 1.

Introduction to Medicine leads into CPC 1 and CPC 2. ICE, Portfolio, Health Systems and Research, MAPS and other longitudinal components run alongside the course blocks rather than replacing them.

↓ Click a course below to see what it covers.

Introduction to Medicine

A shared foundation for what comes next.

A broad introduction to the language and culture of medicine, foundational and social sciences, humanities and professional behaviours.

Student context

The volume increases quickly. Use this block to test study systems without assuming that someone else's routine has to become yours.

Concepts, Patients and Communities 1

The first systems-based course.

Pediatrics, dermatology, immunology, microbiology, blood, health promotion, cardiovascular and respiratory medicine.

Student context

This is a longer course made up of distinct instructional sections. Assessment dates and teaching details belong in MedSIS and Elentra.

Concepts, Patients and Communities 2

The integrated approach continues.

Endocrinology, gastroenterology and genitourinary medicine, continuing the patient-centred, systems-based approach.

Student context

The year is well underway by this point. Keep clinical-skills practice steady rather than leaving it for the end.

Running alongside the blocks

The course name does not describe your whole week.

ICE: Health in Community

What Community Engaged Experiences involve.

A CEE is community-engaged learning within ICE: Health in Community. It is separate from Friday-morning Clinical Skills and is not simply volunteering or clinical shadowing. Students learn with community organizations, connect the experience to health and community and reflect on it through ICE: HC.

Confirmed course source ICE: HC Manual and CEE Guide – Student · revised September 2025

The checklist below is the confirmed requirement for 2T9. Incoming cohorts should use their own current manual for any changed visit counts, dates or forms.

1 visitLong-term care
1 visitIntellectual and developmental disabilities
4 visitsFlexible CEEs
5+ visitsOne longitudinal community placement
Arranging and approving a placement

Students arrange their own CEEs. St. George students complete placements in Toronto or the GTA, excluding Mississauga. MAM students complete placements in Mississauga or the GTA, excluding Toronto. An exception requires advance approval from the ICE: HC Program Director.

If an opportunity is not already listed in MedSIS, send the agency and supervisor contact information to the central ICE: HC office so the placement and paperwork can be reviewed. The manual advises allowing at least three weeks.

What students are expected to do
  • Contact community supervisors professionally and respect the agency's capacity and requirements.
  • Log every placement in MedSIS.
  • Complete the required preparation and documentation in the current course materials.
  • Present and reflect on CEE learning during tutorials using the sign-up process provided by the course.
  • Track your own progress. Tutors monitor progress but do not arrange placements for students.
Absences, changes and current-year details

Use the current ICE: HC manual and course announcements for deadlines, absence procedures, placement changes, forms and any make-up expectations. Contact your tutor and the central ICE: HC office early when something changes rather than relying on a previous cohort's process.

Source: ICE: HC Manual and CEE Guide – Student, revised September 2025, especially the overview and CEE requirements on pp. 4 and 17–18. Shadowing · EEE

Shadowing is organized through Enriching Educational Experiences.

EEEs are part of ICE: Career Advising and Preparation. They include short-term, self-initiated clinical placements used to explore specialties, practice settings and professional roles. Every clinical shadowing experience during Foundations must be logged in MedSIS in advance.

36 hoursMinimum before clerkship

6 hoursMaximum Alt-EEE contribution
These figures come from the ICE: Career Advising and Preparation Student Manual 2025–2026, pp. 9–14. Confirm them against the 3T0 manual when it is released. The manual recommends completing the requirement well before the deadline. See the Faculty’s official ICE:CAP page ↗; the detailed manual itself is distributed in Elentra.
The three official types

How an EEE can be structured

These are the categories used in the 2025–26 manual. They are not interchangeable, and only a limited number of Alt-EEE hours can count toward the requirement.

Core EEE

Clinical career exploration

A self-directed clinical observation arranged directly with a physician supervisor in Canada.

Longitudinal Experience

Sponsored clinical experience

A clinical experience sponsored by a department, division or specialty. FMLE is part of the core curriculum and does not count toward EEE hours.

Alt-EEE

Non-clinical career exploration

Eligible activities such as informational interviews with physicians. Research does not count. The 2025–26 manual permits up to six Alt-EEE hours.

Settings worth exploring

Different settings answer different questions

These are practical examples rather than separate official EEE categories. The experience still needs to be eligible, approved and logged.

ClinicLongitudinal decisions, follow-up and patient relationships
WardInpatient care, rounds and interprofessional teamwork

Emergency departmentUndifferentiated presentations and acute decisions

Before you attend
  1. Read the current CAP and EEE guidance.Confirm the requirements for your cohort before contacting a supervisor.
  2. Arrange the experience.Identify an attending or faculty physician as the primary supervisor.
  3. Log it in MedSIS in advance.An email agreement alone is not an approved EEE.
  4. Complete the site's requirements.This can include registration, training, identification or other local steps.
  5. Return to MedSIS afterward.Record the completed hours and any required documentation.
Timing and eligibility
When can I start?

The 2025–26 manual says students may begin organizing EEEs after academy-specific clinical-work orientation and the first ICE:CAP session.

How much notice is needed?

The manual asks students to give academies at least one week for affiliated experiences. Non-TAHSN hospitals and other clinics require at least three weeks so an affiliation agreement can be checked or established.

When can an EEE happen?

Self-directed time, evenings, weekends and the summer between Years 1 and 2 may be used. An EEE cannot replace mandatory curricular time and must still be logged.

Where can it happen?

The 2025–26 manual limits EEEs to Canada and requires an appropriate affiliation agreement. Check the current manual before choosing a date.

A sample first email

Short, specific and easy to answer.

Hello Dr. [Name],

My name is [Name] and I am a first-year U of T medical student interested in learning more about [specialty or setting]. I was particularly interested in your work in [specific clinical area].

Would you be open to having me shadow you for a half-day in [clinic / on the ward / in the OR] sometime in [date range]? I am happy to work around your availability and complete the required U of T and site registration steps.

Thank you for considering my request,
[Name]

An agreement is not yet an approved EEE. Log the experience in MedSIS, allow enough time for review and complete any site-specific requirements before attending.
