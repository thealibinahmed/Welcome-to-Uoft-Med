The Shape of the Program

Four years, two phases.

The MD program has two broad phases: Foundations in Years 1 and 2, followed by Clerkship in Years 3 and 4. This is an orientation, not a replacement for the official curriculum or academic calendar.

Years 1–2

Foundations (Pre-Clerkship)

Focus
Foundational and clinical sciences taught through ITM, CPC 1, CPC 2 and CPC 3, alongside longitudinal learning in ICE, Portfolio and MAPS.
Typical week
A variable mix of CBL, lectures, independent learning, MAPS, ICE and occasional Portfolio sessions. See the Academics page for a sourced sample week.
Evaluation
Progression depends on satisfactory completion of the program's required assessments and learning activities. Mastery Exercises are a major part of Foundations assessment, while clinical skills are assessed separately. Use official course and assessment documents for current requirements.

Years 3–4

Clerkship

Focus
Full-time clinical rotations across core specialties, followed by selectives and electives that support career exploration and residency planning.
Typical week
Schedules vary by rotation and can include early starts, evenings, weekends and call. Use the current rotation material for attendance and scheduling expectations.
Evaluation
Assessment varies by rotation and program requirement. The official curriculum and clerkship materials should be used for the current assessment structure.
Use the official calendar for current requirements. The UofT MD Academic Calendar publishes the program structure, policies and calendar requirements. The Academics page adds a clearly labelled student view of first year.
Onboarding · Identity & Access

Getting set up.

Your JOINid is the temporary credential used before your permanent UTORid is activated. Your TCard is the physical student card. Fall students can currently begin the official process on May 1. Start with UofT's UTORid and TCard guide ↗ because document requirements and office information can change.

01

Enable your JOINid

Your JOINid is a temporary credential UofT issues so you can start completing onboarding tasks before your full UTORid is provisioned. You'll receive instructions to enable it after accepting your offer.

  • Watch your email (including spam) in the days/weeks after accepting your offer
  • Follow the activation steps in the email and set a JOINid password
  • Use your JOINid to log in to UofT systems and start the TCard process
02

Upload your TCard photo and documents

Once your JOINid is enabled, upload your TCard photo and identity documents through the UofT online portal. The TCard site has a documentation tool that tells you exactly what's required based on your status.

  • Photo for your TCard, meeting UofT's photo specs (passport-style)
  • Valid government-issued photo ID, original, not expired
  • Use the official documentation tool on the TCard site to confirm exactly what to bring/upload for your situation
03

Check your email for submission status

UofT reviews the submitted photo and documents, then sends the outcome by email. Processing time varies, so submit early and make sure the email listed in ACORN is current.

  • Approval/denial notifications come from UofT to the email on file in ACORN
  • If denied, you'll be asked to resubmit, which delays everything downstream
  • Leave time to correct and resubmit anything that is not accepted
04

Activate your UTORid

After approval, follow the activation email to set up your permanent UTORid and UofT email. Use the official instructions in that message rather than relying on screenshots from another student.

  • Follow the steps in the email to activate your UTORid and set up your UofT email address
  • Save your new UTORid credentials in a secure password manager
  • From this point on, UTORid is what you use everywhere on campus
05

Pick up your TCard

Once your UTORid is activated, follow the current UofT instructions to collect your physical TCard. Confirm the location, hours and required original identification before leaving home.

Bring with you:

  • Your UTORid or student number
  • A valid government-issued photo ID (original, not expired). Photos or digital copies of ID are not accepted.
  • Use the documentation tool on the TCard site to confirm what's required for your specific status

Foundations

Pre-Clerkship

The first two years of the MD program. Classroom and CBL before full-time hospital rotations.

Foundations integrates basic and clinical sciences with clinical reasoning. Lectures, CBLs, anatomy, simulation labs, ICE (Integrated Clinical Experience), and Portfolio. The four courses inside Foundations are ITM (Introduction to Medicine), CPC1, CPC2, and CPC3 (Concepts, Patients & Communities).

Clerkship

Years 3 and 4

Full-time clinical rotations across core specialties.

The sequence includes core rotations, selectives and electives. Rotation structure and requirements belong in the current MD Academic Calendar and clerkship materials rather than a student summary.

CBL

Case-Based Learning

Small-group sessions structured around clinical cases.

CBL structure, group size, preparation and attendance expectations can vary by course and cohort. Use the current instructions in Elentra rather than an earlier cohort's routine.

ICE

Integrated Clinical Experience

The longitudinal clinical thread woven through all of Foundations.

ICE includes several streams that run alongside the course curriculum: Clinical Skills, Health in Community, Career Advising & Preparation, and in Year 2 the Family Medicine Longitudinal Experience. In the 2025–26 schedule, Year 1 ICE: Clinical Skills generally occupied Friday morning and Year 2 generally used Thursday morning. Always follow your current MedSIS timetable.

ICE: Clinical Skills

Clinical Skills stream

A recurring morning focused on history-taking, physical examination and clinical encounters.

Sessions may involve simulated or real patients and are organized through your academy. Expectations and locations vary by session, so check the current course material and MedSIS timetable. The clinical skills resource hub is at thehub.utoronto.ca/clinicalskills.

ICE: HC

Health in Community

Two-year community-engaged learning thread inside ICE.

Combines preparation, facilitated learning and experiences with community organizations. The placements are called Community Engaged Experiences (CEEs). Requirements should be taken from the current ICE: HC manual and your assigned course materials.

ICE: CAP

Career Advising and Preparation

Longitudinal mentorship + career advising thread.

Introduces career exploration, specialty information and practical planning. It also provides the program context for arranging and documenting approved shadowing experiences. Follow current ICE: CAP and MedSIS instructions because processes can change.

Portfolio

Reflective Practice

Small-group reflective practice with an Academy Scholar. Not shadowing.

Portfolio includes scheduled small-group sessions, reflective work, self-assessment and progress-review meetings. Dates and submission requirements vary by year. Use the current Portfolio handbook and the submission platform identified by the Faculty.

ICE: FMLE

Family Medicine Longitudinal Experience

Year 2 longitudinal placement in a family medicine practice.

Begins in second year. Pairs you with a family physician you visit periodically across the year, building continuity with a community-based practice. Different from EEE shadowing (which is shorter and self-initiated) and from ICE: HC placements (which are community-organization-based, not physician-based).

CEE

Community Engaged Experience

The actual placement visits inside ICE: HC.

The current ICE: HC manual describes the required CEE types, preparation, documentation and geographic rules. Because these requirements can change between cohorts, this guide explains the term but does not reproduce the checklist.

EEE

Enriching Educational Experiences

Self-initiated shadowing. Short observerships you arrange yourself.

An EEE is an approved opportunity to observe a physician or clinical setting. Students receive an orientation before participating. Registration, site approval and completion requirements must be confirmed in the current ICE: CAP materials before arranging an experience.

OSCE

Objective Structured Clinical Examination

Practical exam where you rotate through stations performing clinical tasks.

OSCE formats, station lengths and timing vary. Use the Faculty's assessment instructions for your year. Practising histories, examinations and communication with classmates can help you prepare.

Academy

Clinical Home Base

Your home base for clinical learning. For 3T0, U of T MD has four academies across St. George and Mississauga.

For the 3T0 entering class, the four academies are FitzGerald, Peters-Boyd, Wightman-Berris and the Mississauga Academy of Medicine. Scarborough Academy of Medicine opens for the following entering cycle, 3T1. Your academy helps organize clinical learning, Portfolio and community.

CaRMS

Canadian Resident Matching Service

The organization that administers residency matches in Canada.

CaRMS is most relevant later in medical school. Follow current Faculty advising and the official CaRMS guidance for application and match requirements.

Elentra

Curriculum System

The MD Program's curriculum management system.

Course pages, learning events and shared materials are commonly housed here. Your actual timetable is in MedSIS. The tools section below gives a clearer system-by-system breakdown.

ISAL

Integrated Summary and Application Lecture

A selective overview of important concepts identified by week leads and lecturers for the upcoming ME.

An ISAL is not a complete list of testable content. The format may be teaching, cases, questions or a mixture of these. Check the learning event for the session's specific expectations.

ERAS

Electronic Residency Application Service

The AAMC application service used by applicants to participating U.S. residency programs.

ERAS is an application system, not a matching service. Students considering U.S. residency should seek current advising early and verify requirements through the AAMC ERAS page and the relevant match organization.

MD+

Additional educational opportunities

The Faculty's collection of combined degrees, graduate credentials and other educational opportunities available alongside or integrated with the MD.

Programs differ in topic, workload, eligibility and deadline. Some recruit early in first year. See the Academics page's opportunity guide, then confirm details through the official MD Plus overview.

MCCQE

Medical Council of Canada Qualifying Examination

A national examination assessing knowledge and clinical decision-making at the level expected of a student completing a Canadian medical degree.

Eligible students can apply up to 15 months before their expected graduation date, with the eligibility window beginning 12 months before expected graduation. Use the MCC eligibility page for current rules, sessions and application instructions.

The Academies

Four academies for 3T0.

Your academy assignment is provided by the MD Program and anchors much of your clinical learning. Student-reported for 3T0: the incoming cohort was described as approximately 306 students across the four academies. Cohort and academy sizes vary by year.

Student-reported timing: 2T9 received its academy email on June 26, 2025, around 10 AM. This is not a published annual deadline and should not be used to predict the 3T0 release date. Consider waiting for your confirmed campus and academy before making location-sensitive housing decisions.

For MAMers

A MAMer's guide to MAM.

If you've been placed at the Mississauga Academy, your daily geography is different. This is the practical stuff: buildings, parking, and how to get downtown when you need to.

Buildings you'll actually use.

UTM is a big campus. For MAM students, the practical map is small: three buildings within a two-minute walk of each other, plus the hospitals.

TDHSC Terrence Donnelly Health Sciences Complex

3359 Mississauga Rd · confirm current access hours

Your home building. The big lecture room on the first floor is where your class meets; small-group rooms on the second floor are reserved for MD and OT students. The cadaver lab is on the fourth floor.

The second-floor lounge

  • Seating, microwave, tea
  • Foosball and ping pong
  • Shared with OT students

Insider: The ping pong table was crowdfunded by the 2T9 MAMers. Recent addition. Late-night study sessions here are a thing.

William G. Davis Building

1867 Inner Cir Rd · 2 min walk from TDHSC

The hub of UTM. Food court, coffee, and the gym you'll have access to (also where intramurals happen). When you need to leave TDHSC for a minute, you come here.

CCT Building Communication, Culture & Technology

1800 Middle Rd · 1 min walk from TDHSC

Houses the UTM Library (a nice change of scenery from TDHSC), plus a few food options and coffee. Prayer room on the second floor.

Queensway Health Centre

150 Sherway Dr, Etobicoke · Trillium's third site

You won't have clinical skills sessions here, but you may shadow here. Some third-year clerkship rotations for MAM also take place at Queensway.

Getting around Mississauga.

A few logistics that catch every new MAM cohort off guard.

Parking

The MAM-specific pass.

Parking options, eligible lots, prices and permit dates can change each year. Watch for the MAM or UTM parking instructions sent to your cohort before purchasing a permit.

Use the permit you actually purchased. UTM's current parking page lists P4, P8 and P9 among student options, with different prices and access rules. A permit is valid only for its designated lot or lots.

Check UTM's current student-permit page ↗ before buying. Parking at the hospitals works differently; follow the current hospital or academy instructions.

MiWay

Included transit access.

Eligible MAM students paying the U-Pass fee receive a MiWay U-Pass. Eligibility is tied to current registration and the fee on your ACORN invoice. Confirm current routes in MiWay rather than relying on an older route number.

UTMSU Digital U-Pass eligibility ↗

For clinical skills mornings.

Getting to St. George.

You may need to travel downtown for O-Week and occasional events during the year. These are four commonly used options.

UTM Shuttle Bus Campus-to-campus option

Included for eligible UTM students · Travel time is traffic-dependent

The UTM–St. George shuttle is often the simplest downtown option. Bring your TCard and check the official schedule before every trip because routes, stops and service hours can change by term.

Two things to know

  • The recent MAMer’s Guide reports that student access activates around the start of the school year. Confirm current activation timing before relying on the shuttle
  • Download the First View app for live arrivals and cancellations

Schedule: Always check utm.utoronto.ca/shuttle. Service changes between summer, term and exam periods.

GO Train

Paid fare · Often useful when shuttle timing does not work

Lakeshore West and Milton-line stations serve different parts of Mississauga. Compare the full door-to-door trip in a current transit planner, including the connection from Union Station to the Medical Sciences Building.

Before travelling

  • Check the current GO schedule and service alerts
  • Include the final local-transit connection, not just the train ride
  • Check your eligibility and apply for the PRESTO post-secondary discount

TTC via Kipling Station Line 2 connection

Paid fare · Requires a connection between Kipling and UTM

From downtown, travel west on Line 2 to Kipling, then connect to UTM using the current MiWay route or another ride. Check the full trip in a live transit planner because subway and bus service change by time and day.

Uber

Paid ride · Cost and travel time vary

A direct ride can be useful when transit service is limited. If travelling with classmates, coordinate the pickup point and confirm the fare before booking.

Questions about MAM logistics? Start with your current MAM Student Affairs contact for campus, academic or logistical questions.

Memberships & Insurance

OMSA and OMA, briefly.

Two organizations you'll hear about often: one for medical students, one for practicing physicians (with a student tier). Both come with practical perks beyond advocacy.

OMSA

Ontario Medical Students Association

Ontario's provincial medical-student voice to the OMA. Students from each Ontario medical school elect representatives to its council.

  • Provincial advocacy, services and student opportunities
  • Events, educational resources, grants and mentorship initiatives vary by year
  • Ask your VP External how UofT students can participate
omsa.ca ↗
OMA

Ontario Medical Association

The provincial association representing physicians, residents and medical students. Its medical-student membership page listed membership at $10 per year when checked in July 2026.

  • Access to member programs, services and discounts
  • Student participation in OMA advocacy and professional activities
  • Eligibility for the separate OMA Insurance Essentials for Students offering, which currently includes discounted disability and complimentary life insurance

The confusing part is rarely logging in. It is knowing which system holds what. This is a practical orientation only; follow the platform named in the Faculty's current instructions when a task is due.

Curriculum hub

Elentra

The MD Program's curriculum platform. Start here for course pages and learning-event material.

  • Open course and learning-event pages
  • Access posted slides, readings and cases
  • Review course announcements and instructions
meded.utoronto.ca/medicine ↗

Student record

ACORN

UofT's student information system, used across all faculties. Where you handle anything tied to your enrolment, fees, and official records. Most MD-specific academic content lives elsewhere; ACORN is for the bureaucracy.

  • View tuition invoices + pay fees
  • File a tuition fee deferral if you have OSAP
  • Update your address and emergency contact
  • Download tax forms (T2202)
acorn.utoronto.ca ↗

Clinical / placements

MedSIS

The MD Program system where your actual timetable lives. Use it for session times and locations, plus the MD-specific tasks assigned to you there.

  • Check your weekly timetable and session locations
  • Complete assigned registration or orientation modules
  • Follow current placement and experience instructions
medsis.utoronto.ca ↗

Portfolio

OASES

A submission system used for assigned MD Program work, including Portfolio submissions when directed. It is not your timetable.

  • Submit the work identified in current Faculty instructions
  • Use the deadline and naming conventions provided for your cohort
medit.med.utoronto.ca/OASES ↗

Marks & progression

Learner Chart

A learner-facing view used for assessment and progression information made available by the MD Program.

  • Review released assessment information
  • Follow Faculty instructions for interpreting your record
medit.med.utoronto.ca/learner-chart ↗

Quizzes & review

ExamSoft

An assessment portal used when identified by the Faculty. Availability and review functions depend on the assessment.

  • Follow the assessment-specific access instructions
  • Check Faculty communications for result-release details
ei.examsoft.com/GKWeb/login/utorontomed ↗

Exam app

Examplify

The secure application used for assessments when directed by the Faculty. Install and test the required version using the instructions sent for your cohort.

  • Complete required setup before the stated deadline
  • Run the assigned practice or system check
  • Use the Faculty's technical-support pathway if setup fails

U of T central LMS

Quercus

Quercus is U of T's central Canvas-based learning system. It is not the routine home for the core MD curriculum, which is organized through Elentra. Open Quercus when a Faculty or course organizer specifically directs you there.

  • Use a Quercus course or module when you receive a direct link or enrolment notice
  • Do not assume that missing Elentra content has moved here
q.utoronto.ca ↗
